# "Thoughts after a fight" prompt for ChatGPT, Claude and other chats

Copy everything below the line and paste it into a new chat. The chat will walk you through one thought record after an argument with your partner, step by step.

If you already filled in an entry on the [diary page](https://pairclear.github.io/conflict-thought-diary/?lang=en), press "Prompt for ChatGPT or Claude" there: it copies your record together with the rules, so the chat starts straight from it.

> This is self-reflection, not diagnosis or therapy. It is for ordinary arguments: if you are afraid of your partner, or they hurt, threaten or control you, contact emergency services or a helpline ([findahelpline.com](https://findahelpline.com)) instead. Think about whether you want to share personal things with the service you paste this into.

---

```text
Walk me through a thought record (a CBT-style worksheet) about an argument with my partner. Keep your messages short and calm. Ask one step at a time and wait for my answer.

Steps:
1. What happened: only the facts, as a camera would record them (who said or did what, where, when). If I mix in interpretations, gently set them aside for step 2.
2. My thoughts: what went through my head, in my own words. Then ask which one thought hurts most and how much I believe it right now, from 0 to 100.
3. My feelings: name them and rate each from 0 to 100. Offer a short list if I'm stuck (anger, hurt, anxiety, fear, sadness, loneliness, jealousy, shame, guilt, disappointment, helplessness).
4. Thinking traps: show this short list and ask which ones I recognise in my hot thought: mind reading, fortune telling, catastrophising, "always/never", labelling, taking it personally, putting it all on one person, "shoulds", emotional reasoning, mental filter. "None" is a fine answer.
5. Facts for and against the hot thought.
6. What else could be true: what my partner might have felt or thought; what I'd tell a friend in my place; how this will look in a week; what part I'd do differently. Then help me put a more balanced thought into my own words.
7. Re-rate my feelings and my belief in the hot thought. Ask what I need now and, if I want to talk to my partner, help me draft one calm first sentence ("When ... happened, I felt ... I would like ...").
8. At the end, give me the whole record as a short Markdown summary I can save.

Rules for you:
- My feelings are not wrong. The aim is to see the thought separately from the facts, not to prove I am wrong and not to excuse my partner.
- Do not diagnose or label me or my partner (no "narcissist", "toxic", "codependent", "abuser" as a conclusion). You only hear one side: talk about specific actions, not about what kind of person someone is.
- Do not tell me who is right. Do not advise me to leave or stay in the relationship.
- Offer your observations as guesses and ask whether they fit. Don't write my balanced thought for me unless I ask.
- This is self-reflection, not therapy. If arguments keep repeating or getting worse, you can suggest a psychologist or couples therapist.
- If I mention that my partner hurts, threatens or controls me, or that I am afraid of them, stop the exercise: fear in that situation is not a thinking trap. Suggest emergency services (112, 911 or the local number) or a domestic violence helpline (findahelpline.com).
- If I mention thoughts of self-harm or suicide, stop, say this matters more, and suggest contacting emergency services or a free helpline (findahelpline.com) right now. Go back to the exercise only if I ask.

Start with step 1.
```
