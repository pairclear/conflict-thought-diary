# Thinking traps in a fight

[Open in the diary →](https://pairclear.github.io/conflict-thought-diary/?lang=en&view=traps) · [Русская версия](thinking-traps.ru.md)

Common thought patterns described in cognitive behavioural therapy. Names and lists differ between authors; this is our own short version with examples from couple conflicts. Everyone falls into them, especially when hurt. Noticing a trap does not mean your feelings are wrong.

> This is not a diagnosis. Noticing a trap does not mean your feeling is wrong or that you are to blame. If you are afraid of your partner, fear is not a trap: see the [safety note](README.md).

## 1. Mind reading

You feel sure you know what your partner thinks or feels, though they did not say it.

- **Example:** "He's quiet, so he's angry with me"
- **Ask yourself:** What exactly did they say or do? Could I ask?

## 2. Fortune telling

A bad outcome feels already decided.

- **Example:** "We'll fight again and it will end in a breakup"
- **Ask yourself:** How often have my predictions come true exactly?

## 3. Catastrophising

An unpleasant event feels like a disaster that can't be repaired.

- **Example:** "That's it, the relationship is ruined"
- **Ask yourself:** What is the most likely outcome, not the worst one?

## 4. Always and never

One event turns into a rule.

- **Example:** "You never listen to me"
- **Ask yourself:** Were there times it was different?

## 5. Labelling

A verdict on the person instead of a description of the action.

- **Example:** "Selfish", "toxic", "narcissist"
- **Ask yourself:** Which specific action hurt me?

## 6. Taking it personally

Your partner's mood or action feels like a response to you.

- **Example:** "They're in a bad mood, so I must have done something wrong"
- **Ask yourself:** What other reasons could there be for their mood?

## 7. All on one person

The whole fight is put on one person: your partner or you.

- **Example:** "It's all their fault" or "It's all me"
- **Ask yourself:** Which part is mine, and which is theirs?

## 8. Shoulds

Rigid rules about how your partner or you "should" behave.

- **Example:** "If he loved me, he should guess what I need"
- **Ask yourself:** Did I say it out loud?

## 9. Emotional reasoning

A strong feeling is taken as proof.

- **Example:** "I feel abandoned, so I am being abandoned"
- **Ask yourself:** What do the facts say if I set the feeling aside for a minute?

## 10. Mental filter

You notice only the bad part and miss the rest.

- **Example:** Out of the whole evening you remember only one sharp remark
- **Ask yourself:** What else happened that day, that week?
